What to look for

  1. Start with Diamond. The first layer forms x₁⁺, (−x₁)⁺, x₂⁺ and (−x₂)⁺. The output combines them into 1 − |x₁| − |x₂|.
  2. Try Diamond ring or Two islands. Some second-layer activation boundaries are already bent in the original input space, even though each neuron takes an affine combination of its own inputs.
  3. Switch off ReLUs. The whole network becomes affine. The final boundary becomes a straight line or degenerates to a constant score; symmetric presets can cancel exactly.
  4. Edit weights and biases. First-layer edits move straight gates. Later-layer edits recombine existing features, changing the shape of the final region.
Definitions, numerical limits, and preset construction

For a hidden neuron, z = Σᵢ wᵢaᵢ + b and a = max(0,z). Its gate boundary separates positive from nonpositive preactivation. A later-layer z is piecewise affine in the original coordinates (x₁,x₂), so this boundary can bend, split, or enclose a region. If z is zero over an area, only its active/inactive frontier is drawn.

The final neuron uses an affine score of the last hidden layer, with no ReLU or sigmoid applied. Class 1 means score > threshold; equality belongs to class 0. A sigmoid with its corresponding probability threshold would give the same partition.

These are hand-designed networks, not trained fits. Extra preset neurons have zero parameters, making the useful subnetwork easy to isolate. Blank hidden plots can mean always active or always inactive within the visible range; a zero neuron is not necessarily a training failure. Disabling ReLUs changes a hidden plot into a positive/negative preactivation map.

Plots use a 137 × 137 numerical grid and interpolated contours. Small features can be missed, and ReLU plateaus may place a contour within one grid cell of its exact location. The mathematical boundaries of a finite ReLU network are piecewise linear; apparent smoothness comes from drawing. The displayed region is a finite window, not all of ℝ².

Architectures can be changed independently of presets. Reloading a preset restores its exact architecture, all weights and biases, enabled ReLUs, zero threshold, default probe, and input range. Every adjacent-layer pair remains fully connected, including weights currently equal to zero. All computation stays in this file and works offline.
